# T3P Standalone

[日本語](README.ja.md)

Experimental Rust firmware for the ATORCH T3P USB power meter, building on [KWS-X1 Standalone](https://github.com/kralonur/kws-x1-standalone-rs) and the local DOOM on T3P board research.

## Status

The default build is a **startup and display demo with simulated readings**. The experimental measurement build reads real values without device-specific calibration. In the KWS-X1-style square UI, **OVERVIEW** shows voltage, signed current, power and their peaks. **ENERGY** shows charge, energy and elapsed time, and **GRAPH** shows the past 1, 2, 5 or 10 minutes of power, voltage or current. A separate profiles build listens for SPR and EPR source offers; both have been captured on the device in the test setups described below. Its PD SNIFF log was compared with an inline CY4500-EPR: some captures matched and others missed messages.

The ENERGY page shows elapsed time since startup or the last total reset, counting even when the reading is missing or current is zero. The user confirmed on hardware that the prior UI's time advances and resets with Ah and Wh. The display stops at `99:59:59` (not tested on hardware); totals and elapsed time are not retained on restart. The separate PD-diagnostic build displays the raw diagnostic bytes in the OVERVIEW protocol card.

The user confirmed the KWS-style layout, double presses and longer graph windows on hardware. Peaks are cleared at power-off. A short left-button press and release cycles OVERVIEW, ENERGY, power graph, voltage graph, and current graph. On a graph page, a double press cycles 1, 2, 5 and 10 minutes. On OVERVIEW or ENERGY, a double press advances one page. A hold of about one second resets Ah, Wh, elapsed time and peaks. On PD SNIFF and CABLE, holding returns to OVERVIEW and preserves the totals. A short press is recognized after a 350 ms double-press interval. Current graphs use magnitude. Graph history continues across pages and is not cleared by reset. Peak values can come from different samples, so multiplying maximum voltage and current need not equal maximum power.

The user confirmed the KWS-style line graph on the device. Each range is reduced to 60 points, representing averages over 1, 2, 5 or 10 seconds respectively. A partially missing interval is drawn as an isolated point, while a completely missing interval leaves a gap. The graph labels zero and its rounded-up vertical maximum in W, V or A. The limit follows the largest plotted average within the selected window, rounded up to a 1, 2, 2.5 or 5 step, with a minimum of 1 W, V or A. Changing the scale can shift the entire trace.

## PD profiles trial

In the `--pd-profiles` build, tap past the current graph to reach **PD PROFILES**. Double press to arm a 10-second capture on CC1 and reconnect the source in a 5 V test setup. Powering the T3P separately through HID lets it listen before the source is connected. Every capture uses CC1; another double press restarts capture on CC1. The tested device has only been observed to receive on CC1, and a cable orientation that uses the other line may not be captured. This software restriction does not establish the internal wiring. The screen keeps the first CRC-checked SPR Source_Capabilities offer in RAM until a new SPR capture or a power cycle. The user confirmed a five-object SPR offer on the T3P.

Tap again for **EPR OFFER**. Double press to listen on CC1 for 30 seconds, then use a connected PD sink such as the ASD-PD31 to query or enter EPR while starting from 5 V. A further double press restarts the CC1 capture. EPR_Source_Capabilities are normally divided into two chunks. `CAPTURED` requires both CRC-valid chunks in sequence. If only chunk 1 is received, `PARTIAL` shows the EPR PDOs present in that chunk. `NO EPR CAPS` means no complete offer was captured during the window. The screen also counts CRC-valid SOP records (`RX`), SPR source-capabilities records (`SPR`) and recognized EPR chunks (`E0` and `E1`), up to 255 each. These counters help distinguish a missing decoded message from an observed EPR offer, but do not prove the absence of traffic on the wire.

Fixed PDO rows show voltage, maximum current and calculated power. PPS shows a voltage range and current limit. SPR AVS shows current limits for 9–15 V and, when advertised, 15–20 V. EPR AVS shows its voltage range and PDP. Other PDO formats appear as raw hex. These are advertised values. The receiver preserves the T3P's startup setting, which enables both internal CC pull-downs on an FUSB302B-compatible chip; PD transmission and automatic GoodCRC are disabled. **It is not a high-impedance passive sniffer and can affect attachment to another sink.** The EPR screen relies on another sink to prompt the offer. The user confirmed one complete EPR capture with a sink connected; other connection states and orientations remain to be verified. [FUSB302B data sheet](https://www.onsemi.com/pdf/datasheet/fusb302b-d.pdf) · [USB Power Delivery specification](https://www.usb.org/document-library/usb-power-delivery)

## PD sniff trial

The next **PD SNIFF** page records CRC-valid SOP messages on CC1 for 30 seconds after a double press. Repeating the double press on this page restarts capture on CC1 and clears the old log. Up to 192 history rows are kept in RAM. The newest eight appear first, in chronological order; a short press shows the preceding eight, and a press on the oldest page advances to CABLE. Hold for about one second to leave immediately without resetting totals. `R:185-192/192`, for example, identifies the displayed range within retained messages; older messages are overwritten when the buffer fills. While listening, new messages can shift the displayed range, so inspect older pages after `DONE`. `SRC` / `SNK` reflect the power-role bit in each message, not independent proof of the physical sender. Source capabilities, Request, Accept, PS_RDY, EPR messages and several other common types are named; unrecognized control, data or extended types show `CTRL`, `DATA` or `EXT` plus `T` and the type number. Multiple history pages were observed on hardware; the 192-message overwrite boundary and long-press exit remain unverified on hardware.

One-object EPR_Mode messages show `EPR_ENTER`, `EPR_ACK`, `EPR_OK`, `EPR_FAIL` or `EPR_EXIT`. Unknown action bytes appear as `EPR_Axx`; `Ixx` is the message ID and `Dxx` is the raw action-specific data byte in hexadecimal. For Enter this is the sink's operational PDP; for Enter Failed, `D00` means unknown cause, `D01` cable not EPR capable, `D02` VCONN failure, `D03` missing RDO EPR capability, `D04` source unable to enter, and `D05` missing PDO EPR capability. Other values remain raw. These labels decode individual messages and do not establish a complete exchange or the active voltage. Enter, Ack, OK and Exit were observed on hardware and agree with the CY4500 captures; Fail and unknown actions remain unverified on hardware. [EPRMDO definitions](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromiumos/platform/ec/+/6e6e567deac5b5ab8fbe0d706cd82ebeb21e35b4/include/usb_pd.h)

The counters below the history summarize EPR KeepAlive from the sink and KeepAlive_ACK from the source. Those messages and GoodCRC use counters instead of history rows. `MSG` counts all CRC-valid non-GoodCRC SOP messages, including the KeepAlive messages and their ACKs. `GCRC` counts all received GoodCRC packets. Each displayed counter tops out at 999. [Extended Control definitions](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromiumos/platform/ec/+/6e6e567deac5b5ab8fbe0d706cd82ebeb21e35b4/include/usb_pd.h#667)

The suffix `N` gives the number of data objects, `P` the requested object position, `C` an extended-message chunk, `Q` a chunk request, and `I` the message ID. `DONE` means the capture window ended, not that every packet was received or a contract was established. The EPR and sniff receivers normally poll at a minimum interval of 1 ms and can immediately drain up to four queued records per pass. After any record is consumed, the next pass skips the normal sleep once; if no further record is consumed, normal waiting resumes. Actual timing also depends on I²C and display work. A read-clear FIFO alert is retained as `WARN` while capture continues; on an FUSB302B-compatible device this alert can indicate RX or TX full, but does not by itself prove data was lost. An RX-full status snapshot stops capture with `FIFO FULL`. An invalid frame or link reset also stops capture and leaves its status visible. Reception can miss traffic, especially during rapid exchanges, and the log does not infer a negotiated voltage or current. This screen uses the same CC pull-down configuration described above. [FUSB302B data sheet](https://www.onsemi.com/pdf/datasheet/fusb302b-d.pdf)

Warnings are retained separately for SPR, EPR, sniff and cable results in RAM, and cleared when a new capture of the same kind starts or the device restarts.

The current receive-priority trial pauses automatic screen refresh during EPR OFFER, PD SNIFF and CABLE captures, to reduce time spent away from the receiver. The screen shows a snapshot until capture finishes, including if another page is selected. Button actions still redraw, and measurement sampling and totals continue. For a comparison, wait for the listening screen to settle before reconnecting the test setup, and leave the buttons alone until capture ends. SPR capture retains its normal refresh. The user tested the preceding `PD_RX_PRIORITY` firmware on hardware, with results below; the screen-freeze behavior itself has not been separately verified. Packet loss remains possible.

## CABLE trial

Open **CABLE** after PD SNIFF and double press the left button to listen on CC1 for up to 30 seconds. Keep the T3P powered via HID and reconnect the source with a known e-marked cable in the 5 V test setup once `LISTENING` appears. The source supplies VCONN and queries the cable. The T3P listens for the first CRC-valid SOP′ Discover Identity ACK from a passive or active cable. `SOP1 RX` counts CRC-valid SOP′ records, including requests and GoodCRC, up to 255.

After `CAPTURED` or `CAPTURE WARN`, a short press advances through **SUMMARY**, **RAW** and **FLAGS**, then returns to OVERVIEW. The summary shows the type, advertised USB speed and power rating, latency-based length estimate, VID/PID and XID. USB PD 3.x / SVDM 2.x passive Cable VDO 1.0 and active Cable VDO 1.3 are decoded. Other layouts retain the identity and raw VDOs for inspection. Double press to replace the capture, or hold to return to OVERVIEW. The result is kept in RAM.

The test fixture reproduces the Discover Identity exchange from the CY4500-EPR capture of the Sumitomo cable. Replay yields a passive USB4 Gen 3 cable rated 50 V / 5 A with the EPR bit set and VID/PID `20C2/0A14`. [USB Power Delivery specification](https://www.usb.org/document-library/usb-power-delivery) · [KWS-X1 CABLE menu](https://github.com/kralonur/kws-x1-standalone-rs#16-cable)

The CC1 hardware trial showed the same identity on the T3P. The photographed PD header `518F` and all five VDOs match the first SOP′ Discover Identity ACK in the CY4500-EPR recording, at about 5.005 V on VBUS. `SOP1 RX:003` agrees with the request, its GoodCRC and the identity response. `CAPTURE WARN` indicates a FIFO alert during a capture that successfully decoded the identity with a matching CRC.

## CC1 Rd attach test

Build with `python build.py --pd-rd-test` and tap past the current graph to open **RD TEST**. Power the T3P through HID and start with both measurement ports unplugged. Double press to arm `RD OFF`. While it is displayed, plug a known USB-C source into the T3P receptacle in the working CC1 orientation. Leave the other measurement port empty. Wait two seconds, then double press to enable Rd. Compare the two saved VBUS readings. `5V SEEN` means VBUS was below 4 V with Rd off and then measured 4.5–5.5 V on three consecutive samples with Rd on.

The first step times out after 30 seconds and the second after 3 seconds. Hold to cancel or tap to leave. The test checks the startup configuration and writes only `SWITCHES0` (`04` then `05`), restoring `03` when the test ends. For `VBUS PRESENT`, unplug the source and start again. For `RESTORE ERROR`, unplug the source and power-cycle the T3P. The test uses the meter's uncalibrated VBUS reading. [FUSB302B data sheet](https://www.onsemi.com/pdf/datasheet/fusb302b-d.pdf)

## Experimental board build

The board build uses Rust 1.99.1 (`58cf8f9c3`), its `thumbv7em-none-eabihf` core, [Pi32v2 LLVM 23.1.5-2](https://github.com/jvitkauskas/pi32v2-llvm/releases/tag/pi32v2-23.1.5-2), and the JieLi assembler for the entry code. It retargets 32-bit LLVM IR to Pi32v2. This toolchain bridge is experimental. Active-time mode produced an incorrect Wh value in the simulator, so the demo uses elapsed-time mode.

Obtain and unpack your own T3P 3.0.3 firmware using [jl-misctools](https://github.com/kagaimiq/jl-misctools). The unpacked `app.bin` must have SHA-256 `ecd03d659db7c961585ffd1e090a7325a82433a1c270f5964f268ddb5c1bdc0c`. The build extracts the LCD initialization records locally into the ignored `build/` directory.

```sh
python build.py --rustc PATH_TO_RUSTC --llvm PATH_TO_PI32V2_LLVM --jieli-clang PATH_TO_JIELI_CLANG --stock-app PATH_TO_UNPACKED_APP_BIN
cargo run --offline --bin preview -- build/preview.ppm
python check_pi32.py --sim PATH_TO_PI32V2_ISA
```

## Hardware research

Static analysis of the exact 3.0.3 application identifies a software I²C bus on PB2/PB1, address 0x40, and reads of registers 0x02 and 0x01. This resembles an INA226 register layout. Chip identification, physical wiring, calibration, and actual values need verification. `check_stock.py` checks the recorded byte evidence against a locally unpacked stock application.
